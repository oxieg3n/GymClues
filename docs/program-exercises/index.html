Bodyweight deep squat

Quads · Glutes · Adductors

Use the prescribed bodyweight support or equipment variation. Check the original program when its exercise label does not specify the apparatus.

Setup

  1. Choose the named bodyweight, front-loaded, back-loaded or guided-machine setup.
  2. Set feet in a comfortable stance and establish stable whole-foot pressure.
  3. Fit the prescribed support and test safeties or machine catches before adding resistance.

How to do it

  1. Bend the knees and hips together while keeping the trunk controlled.
  2. Lower only as far as the feet and pelvis remain stable and the movement is comfortable.
  3. Press through the whole foot to stand without letting the knees collapse inward.
  4. Finish tall without snapping the knees backward, then repeat or re-rack safely.

Safety tips

  1. Use a clear, nonslip floor and a stance that keeps both feet planted.
  2. Choose a comfortable depth without forcing the knees, hips or back into an end position.

Injury prevention

  1. Warm up the squat pattern and increase resistance in stages.
  2. Use a stance that allows the knees to track in the same general direction as the toes.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Heels lifting or weight tipping forward: adjust stance, depth or load.
  2. Knees collapsing inward: slow down and maintain foot pressure.
  3. Forcing depth after the pelvis or back loses position: shorten the range.

Breathing: Inhale and establish support before lowering; exhale through the upward effort.

Dumbbell Lateral Lunge

Quads · Glutes · Adductors

Setup

  1. Choose the named stepping direction, split stance or approved step platform.
  2. Start with bodyweight or a light load and use a stable hand support when needed.
  3. Set a stride or step height that permits controlled foot, knee and pelvic alignment.

How to do it

  1. Transfer weight onto the working leg and bend through the hip and knee.
  2. For lunges, lower through the prescribed forward, backward or sideways stance; for step-ups, place the whole foot on the step.
  3. Drive through the working foot to rise without a sudden push from the trailing leg.
  4. Return slowly to the start and change sides according to the program.

Safety tips

  1. Choose stable footing and a step height or split stance you can control.
  2. For step-ups, use a platform rated for stepping; do not stand on an adjustable bench unless its manufacturer permits it.

Injury prevention

  1. Start with bodyweight or hand support if balance limits the movement.
  2. Use a stride and range that allow the knee and foot to stay aligned without pain.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Taking an excessively wide step: choose a width you can reverse without a painful groin stretch.
  2. Bent knee collapsing inward: keep it tracking with the planted foot.
  3. Rounding the back to reach lower: reduce depth and hinge through the hip.

Breathing: Inhale while lowering and exhale while driving upward.

Leg swing

Hip flexors · Hamstrings · Glutes · Adductors

Use the prescribed bodyweight support or equipment variation. Check the original program when its exercise label does not specify the apparatus.

Setup

  1. Choose the named stretch or mobility position on a stable surface.
  2. Use padding or a fixed hand support where needed for comfort and balance.
  3. Begin with a small, comfortable range rather than forcing the endpoint.

How to do it

  1. Hold a fixed support and stand tall on one leg.
  2. Swing the other leg gently forward and backward for the sagittal variation, or across and outward for the side-to-side variation.
  3. Keep the pelvis and trunk controlled rather than throwing the leg to maximum height.
  4. Reduce the range if balance changes, then switch sides as prescribed.

Safety tips

  1. Move gently through a comfortable range; stretching should not cause sharp pain or numbness.
  2. Use a stable support when balance is required and keep the area clear.

Injury prevention

  1. Warm up with easy movement before deeper positions.
  2. Increase range gradually without forcing the shoulder, hip or spine into an end position.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Bouncing into a stretch: move slowly and ease out under control.
  2. Holding the breath: maintain relaxed breathing.
  3. Compensating with a large back arch or twist: reduce the range and isolate the intended region.

Breathing: Keep slow, relaxed breathing throughout the movement or hold.

Back extension

Back · Glutes · Hamstrings

Setup

  1. Choose the named supported bench, reverse-hyper machine or prone floor position.
  2. Fit any hip pad and foot restraints before taking bodyweight or external load.
  3. Begin with a short range and a controlled trunk position.

How to do it

  1. For hip-dominant back extensions, hinge through the hips while keeping the back position steady.
  2. For reverse hypers, raise the legs with the torso supported; for Superman, gently raise the prescribed limbs from the floor.
  3. Return toward a straight, comfortable body line without throwing into a large backbend.
  4. Lower slowly and stop before momentum or discomfort determines the range.

Safety tips

  1. Use a comfortable range and avoid throwing the trunk or legs into a large backbend.
  2. For extension benches, fit the hip pad and foot restraints before adding resistance.

Injury prevention

  1. Start with bodyweight and a short arc; add resistance only after control is consistent.
  2. For hip-dominant extensions, let the hips hinge while the back position stays steady.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Swinging through the bottom: slow down.
  2. Lifting far past a straight body line: stop at a controlled finish.
  3. Holding the breath through a long set: breathe while keeping the trunk supported.

Breathing: Exhale through the lifting phase and inhale through the controlled return.

Banded shoulder pass-through

Chest · Back · Shoulders

Requires an intact resistance band with a wide, comfortable grip; never force a pass behind the body.

Setup

  1. Use a sound resistance band and a wide grip that permits comfortable shoulder movement.
  2. Stand tall with stable feet and soft elbows.
  3. Keep the ribs controlled and choose a tension that does not force the shoulders.

How to do it

  2. Move it farther only while the shoulders and trunk remain controlled; a complete pass behind the body is not required.
  3. Reverse along the same gentle path.
  4. Widen the grip or shorten the arc if you need to arch, bend the elbows or push through discomfort.

Safety tips

  1. Move gently through a comfortable range; stretching should not cause sharp pain or numbness.
  2. Use a stable support when balance is required and keep the area clear.

Injury prevention

  1. Warm up with easy movement before deeper positions.
  2. Increase range gradually without forcing the shoulder, hip or spine into an end position.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Bouncing into a stretch: move slowly and ease out under control.
  2. Holding the breath: maintain relaxed breathing.
  3. Compensating with a large back arch or twist: reduce the range and isolate the intended region.

Breathing: Breathe slowly throughout; do not strain or hold the breath to gain range.

Barbell Clean and Press

Glutes · Quads · Back · Shoulders

Setup

  1. Learn the clean and front-rack catch with qualified instruction before adding load.
  2. Use a suitable bar, clear lifting space and a load you can handle through both clean and press.
  3. Rehearse the pull, catch, overhead clearance and controlled return separately.

How to do it

  1. From the coached clean start, drive through the feet and extend the hips to bring the bar upward close to the body.
  2. Receive the bar in a controlled front-rack position rather than catching it on extended wrists.
  3. Stabilize the feet and trunk, then press from the shoulders to a comfortable overhead finish.
  4. Lower to the front rack and return the bar using the controlled sequence taught for the exercise.

Safety tips

  1. Clear the lifting area, secure plate-loaded bars and keep the load close to your body.
  2. For cleans, learn the pull and front-rack catch with a qualified coach before loading; do not improvise a catch with heavy weight.

Injury prevention

  1. Practice the setup and progressively warm up before working sets.
  2. Use a grip you can maintain and stop before the trunk or bar path becomes uncontrolled.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Jerking the load off the floor: take tension and drive smoothly.
  2. Letting the load drift away: keep it close to the legs.
  3. Leaning backward at lockout: finish tall without forcing a backbend.

Breathing: Reset breathing and trunk support between the clean and press phases; learn loaded bracing with a coach.

Barbell row

Back

Setup

  1. Set the prescribed seat, chest support, hand support or hip-hinge position before taking the load.
  2. Take an even grip and reach the arms without losing trunk control.
  3. For a single-arm row, stabilize the pelvis and choose a load you can move without twisting.

How to do it

  1. Draw the elbow or elbows back along the intended row path.
  2. Move the shoulder blades with control rather than shrugging the neck.
  3. Pause at a comfortable finish without lifting off a chest pad or leaning farther backward.
  4. Reach forward slowly while keeping the same supported or braced torso position.

Safety tips

  1. Set your support or foot brace before taking cable or free-weight tension.
  2. Keep the torso controlled; end the set if you cannot maintain your chosen back position.

Injury prevention

  1. Use a lighter load to learn the row without torso momentum.
  2. For single-arm rows, use a stable hand support if balance or rotation limits control.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Heaving the chest upward: reduce weight and keep the torso steady.
  2. Twisting toward the lifting arm: resist rotation through the trunk.
  3. Shrugging every pull: move the elbow back without crowding the neck.

Breathing: Exhale while rowing and inhale through the controlled reach.

Barbell shrug

Back

Setup

  1. Choose the prescribed standing or seated position with weights balanced at the sides or in front.
  2. Take a secure grip and keep the head comfortably above the torso.
  3. Select a load you can hold and set down without losing posture.

How to do it

  1. Elevate the shoulder blades in a smooth upward movement.
  2. Pause without jutting the chin forward.
  3. Lower the shoulders slowly rather than rolling them in circles.
  4. Repeat while grip and neck position remain controlled, then return the weights safely.

Safety tips

  1. Choose a load you can hold and lower safely without grip slipping.
  2. Elevate and lower the shoulder blades without rolling the shoulders in large circles.

Injury prevention

  1. Keep the neck in a comfortable neutral position.
  2. Use smaller loads or shorter sets if grip or posture fails before the shoulders.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Rolling shoulders: move up and down smoothly.
  2. Jutting the chin forward: keep the head over the torso.
  3. Bouncing heavy weights: use a controlled pause and lowering phase.

Breathing: Exhale during elevation and inhale while lowering.

Barbell Upright Row

Shoulders · Back

Setup

  1. Choose the prescribed bar, cable or dumbbell grip that allows comfortable wrists.
  2. Stand with feet stable and the load close in front of the thighs.
  3. Use a light load and decide on a comfortable lift height before starting.

How to do it

  1. Bend the elbows and guide them outward as the load rises close to the torso.
  2. Stop before the shoulder feels compressed or the wrists fold awkwardly.
  3. Keep the torso steady rather than heaving the hips.
  4. Lower slowly along the same close path and repeat without forcing additional height.

Safety tips

  1. Use a comfortable grip and stop the lift before the shoulder feels compressed.
  2. Keep the load close without wrenching the wrists into an awkward bend.

Injury prevention

  1. Begin with a low lift height and increase range only while comfortable.
  2. Choose a cable, dumbbell or wider grip if a fixed bar position does not suit your shoulders.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Pulling elbows as high as possible: stop at a comfortable height.
  2. Heaving the hips: lower the load.
  3. Bending wrists to lift farther: keep the hands aligned with the forearms.

Breathing: Exhale on the lift and inhale on the return.

Chin-up

Back · Biceps

Use a fixed, rated pull-up bar and an underhand grip; additional weight or assistance is optional only when prescribed.

Setup

  1. Use a secure pull-up bar with a clear mounting and landing area.
  2. Choose an overhand grip for a pull-up or an underhand grip for a chin-up; use the width prescribed.
  3. Mount using the intended steps and establish a controlled hang with the trunk braced.

How to do it

  1. Bend the elbows and draw them toward the sides as the body rises.
  2. Lift through a range you can control without kicking or craning the neck.
  3. Pause near the top while maintaining shoulder support.
  4. Lower slowly to a comfortable hang, then dismount using the intended support.

Safety tips

  1. Check the bar and use secure steps to reach it; clear the landing area.

Injury prevention

  2. Build controlled assisted repetitions before adding weight.

Common mistakes & corrections

  2. Craning the neck over the bar: lift with the arms and back.
  3. Dropping abruptly into the hang: control the descent.

Breathing: Exhale through the pull and inhale during the controlled lowering phase.

Weighted or bodyweight triceps dip

Triceps · Chest

Use stable dip bars or the prescribed assisted dip station. Free dip bars and a seated dip machine are different setups.

Setup

  1. Use stable dip bars, or adjust the seat and handles on the prescribed dip machine.
  2. Choose assistance or a light machine load that allows a controlled descent.
  3. Use the intended steps and handles to mount, and establish active shoulder support.

How to do it

  1. Bend the elbows and lower only through a comfortable shoulder range.
  2. Keep the neck relaxed and the wrists controlled.
  3. Extend the elbows to raise the body or press the machine handles down without bouncing.
  4. Control the return and use the designated steps or re-rack procedure to finish.

Safety tips

  1. Check that the bars are stable and choose assistance if you cannot lower and rise smoothly.
  2. Stop the descent before the shoulders roll forward or feel pinched.

Injury prevention

  1. Start with a shallow, controlled range and build depth only as tolerated.
  2. Use steps or a stable platform to mount and dismount; do not jump down with added weight.

Common mistakes & corrections

  1. Dropping rapidly into the bottom: slow the lowering phase.
  2. Shrugging or hanging passively: maintain active shoulder support.
  3. Forcing extra depth: shorten the range rather than chasing an arbitrary elbow angle.

Breathing: Inhale on the lowering phase and exhale through elbow extension.
